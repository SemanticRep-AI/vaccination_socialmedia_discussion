Summary

Both pipelines implement the same study and, where they overlap, they produce identical numbers. The engagement formulas match to the digit and the language filter returns the same four counts. The notebook goes considerably further: it builds the sampling plan, allocates quotas, draws the sample and prepares coder files, none of which the earlier tool attempted.

The quota allocation in the notebook is textbook — proportional shares with largest-remainder rounding, which lands exactly on each month's target — and has been carried through unchanged. Using two engagement measures rather than one is a deliberate and defensible choice. The diagnostics are good, and it was the notebook's own duplication diagnostic that exposed the largest problem in it.

Three issues affect the sample the coders are working from now. Duplicates are removed after the draw rather than before, which cuts each month roughly 43% short of target and strips out 82.9% of the engagement in the sample. Ties on the platform engagement column are broken by position in the file, which turns a top-percentile rule into a recency rule for the dataset where most values are tied. And because no inclusion probability is recorded, nothing drawn can be used to estimate a quantity about the corpus.

All three are fixable without changing the study design. Deduplicate the frame before drawing, break ties with a seeded random draw, and record the inclusion probability of every selected unit. In the merged pipeline those three changes raise the effective sample size from about 200 to 2,008, and the weights reproduce the frame to within two units in 81,266.

Findings

A Deduplication happens after the draw fix

The notebook draws the sample, writes one CSV per month, then removes duplicate content from those files. The intent is right — coders should not read the same text twice — but the ordering has two consequences that compound.

The realised sample is 43% smaller than designed. A month targeted at 2,000 posts delivers 1,111. Across the study, 16,257 posts drawn become 9,267 usable.

The loss is concentrated in exactly the wrong place. Duplicates here are reposts, and a repost inherits the original's impression count, so duplicate groups sit in the upper tail of engagement. Measured on the drawn sample:

PostsMean content_engagementMedian
Kept9,26710,998194
Removed as duplicate6,99070,93232,474

By decile of engagement, the removal rate runs 1%, 0%, 1%, 1%, 10%, 75%, 83%, 87%, 88%, 85%. 82.9% of the engagement mass in the drawn sample is removed — the viral content the design exists to capture. A design that spends its whole budget reaching the tail then discards most of what it found.

The fix is to deduplicate the frame instead. Collapse identical text before drawing, keep the highest-engagement member of each group, and retain the group size as duplicate_count. Quotas are then met exactly, every unit has a known probability of selection, and repost volume survives as a measurable variable rather than being thrown away. Between 26% and 60% of each dataset turns out to be reposts — a finding about the corpus, not a loss in the sample.

B Ties are broken by position in the file fix

stratum.nlargest(n_top, "engagement") keeps the first rows encountered when values are equal. The platform's own engagement column is zero for 86–94% of posts, so the value at a top-5% cutoff is frequently shared by hundreds of them. In anti_vaccine an average of 800 posts per stratum are tied at the cutoff.

The exports are ordered by publication time, so "first in the file" means "most recent". A rule intended to select the most engaged posts silently selects the most recent among the tied ones. Overall 5.2% of top-5%-by-engagement slots are filled this way, rising to 15.3% in anti_vaccine.

# reference
top_engagement_idx = stratum.nlargest(n_top, "engagement").index

# merged: rank on the measure, break ties with a seeded draw
order  = np.lexsort((jitter(stratum.index, seed), -values))
chosen = stratum.index[order[:k]]

This also matters for the estimation in finding C: inclusion probabilities assume tied units are exchangeable, which a positional tie-break violates.

C No inclusion probabilities are recorded fix

The selection rule mixes a near-census of the priority pool with a random draw from the remainder, and in some strata a random draw from within the pool. Each of those has a different and computable probability of selection, but none is written down. Without them the sample can describe itself and nothing else: any statement of the form "X% of vaccine discourse was …" is unsupported, because posts entered the sample at wildly different rates.

Recording inclusion_prob at the moment of selection costs nothing and makes Horvitz–Thompson estimation available. In the merged pipeline the weights reproduce the frame to within two units:

There was also a design flaw worth naming here, which the merged pipeline's own validator caught. Where the priority pool is larger than the quota, the reference rule draws entirely from the pool — so every unit outside it has probability zero and the stratum becomes inestimable. Reserving a fifth of each stratum's quota for a random draw from outside the pool keeps every unit reachable. That single change moved the pooled design effect from 39.4 to 4.05 and the effective sample size from about 200 to 2,008.

D The cap makes the design unequal-probability document

Sample size is 10% of a month's posts capped at 2,000. Two months exceed the cap and are sampled at 9.1% and 7.6% instead. Posts from those months therefore stand for more of the corpus than posts from any other month, and pooling across months without weights under-represents exactly the two highest-volume periods.

The effect is modest — weights range 9.85 to 13.11, a ratio of 1.33 — but it is real and it is invisible in the output. It also has a pleasant resolution: once the frame is deduplicated, no month's 10% exceeds 2,000, the cap stops binding altogether, and the design becomes self-weighting across periods. Fixing finding A dissolves this one.

One naming point belongs here. The function computing this is calculate_monthly_sample_size(summary_table, rate=0.10, cap=2000) and it assigns to a column named two_percent. The rate in use is 10%, not 2%. The code is right and the label is wrong, but anyone reading the output table will conclude the study sampled at 2%.

E The priority branch has almost nothing to prioritise simplify

When the high-engagement pool exceeds the quota, the rule takes posts selected by both measures first and fills the rest from posts selected by one. Across all 51 strata, posts selected by both account for 2.6% of the priority pool, and in 14 strata the overlap is exactly zero.

So the branch is not wrong, it is inert: in practice the rule reduces to a random draw from the union of the two top-5% sets. That is a reasonable design, and stating it that way is clearer than a three-case priority scheme whose first case is almost always empty. The merged pipeline keeps the union and drops the ranking, while still recording how many measures selected each unit so the question stays answerable.

F Smaller notes worth changing

  • Pearson alone understates the agreement. The notebook reports correlations of 0.018 to 0.163 between content_engagement and the platform column, and concludes they are close to unrelated. On a variable this heavy-tailed, Pearson is dominated by a handful of extreme posts. The rank correlation — which is also the form the sampling rule uses, since selection is on order, not magnitude — runs 0.27 to 0.51. The measures are different, but not as unrelated as the Pearson figures suggest, and the higher figure is the one relevant to whether they would pick the same posts.
  • Cells executed out of order. The exported notebook shows (7902, 101) twice for anti_vaccine, which is pro_vaccine's count; the displayed output is stale relative to the code above it. Nothing downstream depends on it, but it means the HTML export cannot be taken as a record of a single clean run. A script that runs top to bottom removes the question.
  • Intermediate state travels through CSV. all_monthly_counts.csv is written, then read back, which silently converts the month from a pandas Period to a string. The sampler compensates with .astype(str). It works, but the pipeline depends on a round-trip through disk for a type coercion.
  • One seed for every stratum. random_state=42 is passed to every .sample() call. Deriving each stratum's seed from a hash of the master seed and the stratum's identity keeps the draws independent while leaving the whole run reproducible from one number.
  • Quotas can exceed a stratum's population. The sampler guards with actual_quota = min(quota, population_n), which is correct locally but leaves the month short of its target. Capping at capacity during allocation and redistributing the surplus to other datasets in the same month keeps the month whole.

What to do

  1. Deduplicate the frame, then redraw. This is the only change that affects conclusions rather than rigour. The current coder sample is missing 83% of the engagement it was designed to capture, and no amount of later weighting recovers it.
  2. Record inclusion probabilities from now on. Two columns. Without them the coded data supports descriptive claims about the sample and nothing about the corpus.
  3. Break ties at random with a fixed seed. A one-line change that removes an unintended recency bias from the dataset where it is largest.
  4. Rename two_percent. Free, and it prevents a methods section reporting the wrong sampling rate.
  5. Report the effective sample size, not the row count. 2,008, not 8,126. It is the honest number for a design that deliberately over-samples the tail, and reviewers will ask.
  6. Keep the allocation exactly as it is. Proportional shares with largest-remainder rounding is correct, lands on the target every month, and needed no change.

If only one thing changes, make it the first. Everything else improves what can be claimed from the data; that one determines what is in it.
